Aims and scope

The Willowbank Watershed Lab studies the movement of water, sediment, carbon and nutrients through river corridors and their floodplain margins. Work in the lab combines long-term field monitoring, controlled flume experiments, and numerical modelling. The publications listed on this page cover peer-reviewed journal articles, conference papers, workshop papers, and occasional technical reports.

Entries are grouped and displayed by the year of publication. Each card shows the title, the author list, the venue, and an optional code badge that links to an archived implementation. The catalogue is regenerated from the per-entry Markdown records in the website repository; the records themselves are the authoritative description of each entry. Where a record points to a code repository, that repository is maintained by the lab and released under an open licence once the associated paper is public.

Readers who need access to field data that cannot be redistributed should contact the corresponding author. The lab follows the regional monitoring agency's data-sharing agreement, and some measurement series are available only to partner organisations. Requests are reviewed by the lab data committee at its quarterly meeting.

This page is maintained by the communications team. Corrections to bibliographic details should be sent to the lab office with the record identifier so that the underlying record can be corrected and the page rebuilt. Please do not send corrections against the rendered HTML: the page is regenerated automatically and manual edits are overwritten on the next build.

Citation metrics shown elsewhere on the site are refreshed from an external index once a month and are not part of the publication records. Where the external index and the local record disagree, the local record is the one maintained by the authors and should be treated as authoritative for the purposes of the website.

Historical entries are retained during site migrations and should not be removed even when a project has ended. The lab keeps an archive copy of the site sources in its institutional repository, and the archived copies are refreshed at the end of each calendar year.

Data and code policy

The lab aims to make analysis code available wherever the associated agreement permits. Code is released through the lab's account on the hosting service; each released project has its own repository with a stable name, a README describing the implementation, and a release marker. A repository is only linked from a publication page once the authors have declared it released. Repositories that are still being prepared for release are kept private or are clearly marked as unreleased, and they should not be cited as the implementation of a paper.

Where a paper has no released implementation, the entry carries no code link at all. This is a deliberate choice rather than an oversight: a link to a partial or unrelated implementation would mislead readers.

Several housekeeping exports (release-status summaries, citation counts, and an annual archive index) are produced by separate scripts and are regenerated on their own schedules. They are not part of the publication records themselves.

Wetland connectivity graphs predict nutrient exchange at floodplain margins

V. Diaz, M. Okonkwo, S. Kowal, J. Mensah

Accepted at the Riverine Systems Symposium 2026 (RSS 2026) · 2025-09-15

conference-paper

We represent a floodplain mosaic as a weighted connectivity graph and show that graph centrality predicts nutrient exchange measured at 22 margin plots. The paper introduces the graph construction and a cross-validation protocol for withheld wetland patches.

Benthic light transects as a proxy for submerged macrophyte stress

V. Diaz, P. Nwosu, A. Quill

Aquatic Optics, 6(2): 77-95 · 2025-04-08

journal-article

We relate light attenuation measured along 56 transects to macrophyte cover and leaf stress indicators. Transects with a diffuse attenuation coefficient above 2.1 per metre showed a clear decline in cover the following season.

Settling velocities of microplastic fibres in slow-moving rivers

V. Diaz, H. Lindqvist, R. Sable

Water Research Advances, 27: 100421 · 2024-10-21

journal-article Code

We measured settling velocities for 1,140 synthetic fibres in a recirculating flume. Fibre length and biofilm growth explained most of the variance; the empirical model reproduces the observed deposition patterns in two lowland rivers.

Environmental DNA scoring for rapid biodiversity assessment

V. Diaz, T. Harrow, J. Mensah

Accepted at the 2024 Workshop on Molecular Monitoring (MMW 2024) · 2024-03-04

workshop-paper

A closed-reference scoring pipeline converts eDNA metabarcoding reads into a site-level biodiversity score in under ten minutes. The score tracks macroinvertebrate richness from paired kick-net samples.

Telemetry reveals fish passage bottlenecks at low-head weirs

V. Diaz, S. Kowal

Accepted at the 2023 Workshop on River Connectivity (WRC 2023) · 2023-12-11

workshop-paper Code

Passive integrated transponder arrays at six low-head weirs show that passage probability falls below 40 percent at two structures during low flow. The workshop paper reports the first season of tag detections.

Speciation of trace metals in anoxic sediment porewater

V. Diaz, N. Achebe, M. Okonkwo

Environmental Chemistry Frontiers, 11: 100377 · 2023-09-27

journal-article Code

We combine diffusive gradients in thin films with a speciation model to partition copper and zinc between sulphide-bound and dissolved phases in anoxic porewater. Sulphide availability explains most of the site-to-site variation.

Methane emissions from restored floodplain meadows

V. Diaz, H. Lindqvist

Wetlands & Climate, 9(1): 55-73 · 2023-05-19

journal-article

Restored floodplain meadows emitted less methane than adjacent grazed pasture in two of three summers, but the difference was not significant in the wettest year. Water-table depth was the dominant control.

A trait-based macroinvertebrate index for wetland condition

V. Diaz, A. Quill, T. Harrow

Ecological Indicators, 143: 109312 · 2022-10-07

journal-article

We build a trait-based index from 214 wetland samples and show that it separates drained from intact wetlands better than abundance-based indices. The index is calibrated against a regional reference set.

Flight planning for repeatable drone surveys of gravel-bed rivers

V. Diaz, P. Nwosu

Proceedings of the 2022 Unmanned Survey Symposium · 2022-02-18

conference-paper

Repeat surveys of the same reach differ mostly because of varying sun angle and overlap. A fixed flight template reduced between-survey elevation error from 9 to 3 centimetres.

Retention of fine sediment in restored riparian buffers

V. Diaz, R. Sable, M. Okonkwo

Freshwater Ecology Letters, 14(3): 88-104 · 2021-06-21

journal-article

Ten restored buffers retained between 31 and 68 percent of the fine sediment delivered from adjacent arable fields during storm events. Retention scaled with buffer width and stem density rather than with age since restoration.

Open-hardware oxygen microsensors for sediment incubations

V. Diaz, T. Harrow

Limnology & Instrumentation, 18(4): 221-240 · 2020-09-03

journal-article

We describe a low-cost oxygen microsensor and a calibration rig for millimetre-scale profiles in intact sediment cores. Profiles agree with commercial sensors to within 4 percent of air saturation.

Riverbank carbon flux responds to seasonal water-table drawdown

V. Diaz, M. Okonkwo, L. Bramble

Journal of River Science, 32(2): 145-162 · 2019-04-12

journal-article

We instrumented four riverbank transects with closed-chamber gas analysers over two hydrological seasons. Carbon flux responded within three days of water-table drawdown, and the response was strongest in coarse-grained banks.
